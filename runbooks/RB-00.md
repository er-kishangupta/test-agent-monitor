## When this fires
- You start a shift, or a new case appears in the queue.

## First 10 minutes
1. Work the Alerts tab top-down: it is sorted by priority (P1 first), then score. Read the **Analyst summary** at the top of the case: verdict, what happened, why it matters, do-now, who to ask.
2. P1 = drop everything (score 90+ and high confidence). P2 = same hour. P3 = same day. P4 = backlog. The *Why P?* line lists every reason; a case capped at P2 is severe but not yet proven.
3. Do the **Do now** action before reading anything else. If it needs a decision, use the Approve / Decline buttons: each decision names an owner and a deadline.

## Is it real?
- Confidence is high when the case is correlated, seen by two or more log sources, shows the action actually happened, or EDR confirmed it. If none of these holds, treat it as unproven and corroborate first.
- Low signals (grey, dashed) are stored but never page anyone. Open them only when hunting or when they sit inside a case.

## Investigate
- Open the rule's runbook from the case (button *Runbook*). Check *Log sources*: a silent source means this case may be only part of the story.
- Check *Known-environment register* for the entry the alert depends on; an `assumed` entry means the rule may be guessing.

## Contain and recover
- Close as true or false positive with a note. If false positive, always ask what the rule did not know and propose a register entry (see RB-GOV).

## Escalate
- P1 or any customer-data exposure: SOC lead immediately, then follow the incident path in the operating model. Never hold a P1 waiting for more evidence.

## False positives: fix the policy, not the analyst
- A pattern of false positives on one rule is a policy gap, not an analyst problem: raise it as a register proposal, not a mental note.

## Close the case
- Set status, owner and a note. Closed as FP means a register proposal or a validation case exists; closed as TP means decisions are Approved or Declined with a reason.
